# Chapter 07 — Knowledge

Version: 0.1
Status: Draft

---

## 1. Purpose

Knowledge is the **organized expertise of the organization**.

It is the curated, durable record of what the organization knows: documents, procedures, specifications, past decisions, reference material. Knowledge is the raw material from which Brains are built and the shared memory an entire workspace can draw on.

Knowledge is deliberately separated from both the Employee (who is not its knowledge) and the Brain (which *packages* knowledge into reusable intelligence). Knowledge is the source; the Brain is one way to apply it.

---

## 2. Responsibilities

The Knowledge base is accountable for:

- **Storing curated expertise** — documents, specs, SOPs, decisions, references.
- **Supporting retrieval** — so that Employees and Brains can find what is relevant.
- **Tracking provenance** — where each piece of knowledge came from and who curated it.
- **Evolving under control** — being revised, not silently mutated.
- **Distinguishing knowledge from memory** — Knowledge is durable and shared; an Employee's working Memory is temporary and personal.

---

## 3. Owns

The Knowledge base owns:

- **Documents and entries** — the actual content.
- **Embeddings and indexes** — the structures that make content retrievable.
- **Sources and provenance** — the origin and curation history of each entry.
- **Curation rules** — what is accepted, how it is tagged, who approves it.
- **Version history** — prior states of the knowledge, so the organization can know what was known *then*.

Knowledge belongs to the **Workspace**. Brains reference it; Employees benefit from it; neither owns it.

---

## 4. Doesn't Own

Knowledge does **not** own:

- **Decisions** — knowing something is not the same as deciding to act on it.
- **Execution** — Knowledge never runs.
- **Working context** — what an Employee is doing right now belongs to that Employee's Memory, not to Knowledge.
- **Persona or prompt** — those belong to the Brain.

Knowledge is the library. It informs; it does not act.

---

## 5. Lifecycle

```
Ingested → Curated → Published → Revised → … → Deprecated → Archived
```

- **Ingested** — a new piece of content has arrived, not yet trusted.
- **Curated** — reviewed, tagged, and structured for retrieval.
- **Published** — available to Brains and Employees.
- **Revised** — updated as the organization learns more; prior versions retained.
- **Deprecated** — superseded or no longer reliable; kept for reference.
- **Archived** — preserved as history.

Not everything ingested should be published. Curation is what separates an organization's knowledge from a pile of files.

---

## 6. Future Extension

The Knowledge concept may grow to support:

- **Conflict resolution** — detecting and reconciling contradictory entries.
- **Provenance chains** — tracing a conclusion back through every source that fed it.
- **Federation** — trusted sharing of knowledge across Workspaces.
- **Staleness detection** — flagging knowledge that may be out of date.
- **Derived knowledge** — knowledge generated and verified by Employees, then promoted into the curated base.
